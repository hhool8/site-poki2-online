The best games are always more fun with another person. But setting up cooperative or competitive play has historically required buying the same game, owning compatible consoles, or paying for online subscriptions. Browser multiplayer games cut through all of that. Share a link, and you're playing together in seconds — from anywhere on the planet, on any device with a browser. Here are the six multiplayer browser games we publish that are genuinely worth your time.

1. Slither.io

Genre: IO / Snake | Players: Hundreds simultaneously

The multiplayer snake game that refuses to die. Guide your snake around a shared arena, eating glowing pellets to grow longer while avoiding other players. The strategy depth comes from how you use your length: coiling around opponents to cut off their escape, or sprinting (at the cost of losing mass) to dodge a larger snake. Slither.io has no login, no time limit, and no cost — you are in a live match within seconds of the page loading, whether or not anyone you know is playing.

2. Shell Shockers

Genre: First-Person Shooter | Players: Room-based

You play as an egg with a gun. Yes, really. Shell Shockers is a first-person shooter where every character and projectile is egg-themed — it's absurd, charming, and genuinely fun. The game runs well even on low-end devices, has multiple weapon classes and maps, and has a dedicated player base that keeps matches full around the clock. It is also the one title here where the theme does real work: the egg armour and the shell shattering give you a visual read on a hit you would otherwise have to infer from a crosshair.

3. Smash Karts

Genre: Multiplayer Racing | Controls: WASD + Mouse

Smash Karts is a 3D kart battle arena game. You drive a kart around a compact arena, collect weapons, and blast opponents — the loop is closer to Splatoon than to Mario Kart, because the point is to survive the pack rather than finish first. Rounds are short, the arena is small enough that you are rarely far from someone else's line of fire, and the combination of driving and shooting means you are constantly deciding which one to prioritise. It is the best choice here for a group that wants to play together on one screen as well as apart.

4. Escape Tsunami Brainrots Online

Genre: Multiplayer Survival | Controls: Mouse / Keyboard

Escape Tsunami Brainrots Online is a race to outrun a massive tsunami wave alongside viral Brainrot characters in a chaotic multiplayer survival run. The wave does not wait, so the pressure is constant, and the character roster gives each run a slightly different feel. It is the most immediately readable title in this list — the objective is literally a wall of water behind you — which makes it the easiest one to drop a non-gamer into.

5. Tic Tac Toe IO

Genre: Puzzle / Duel | Controls: Mouse / Tap

Tic Tac Toe IO takes the oldest game in the world and rebuilds it as a real-time head-to-head duel against opponents worldwide. Because both boards are visible at once, it is simultaneously the most approachable and the most strategically interesting entry here: the opening moves are obvious, and the value comes from the endgame, when blocking becomes a two-way problem. It runs on a phone with taps alone, and a full match takes under a minute, which makes it the natural game to send in a group chat.

6. Basketball Stars

Genre: Sports / PvP | Players: 1 or 2, with online PvP

Basketball Stars is a 1v1 street basketball game with dribbling mechanics, trick shots, and real PvP matches. Character customisation gives each player an identity beyond "player one" and "player two", the shot timing window rewards practice, and the matches are short enough that best-of-five stretches into a lunch break. It works as a head-to-head on one keyboard, but its real strength is as an online match — the shot-timing game translates cleanly into a versus format because both players are working from identical information.

Honourable Mentions

Six games barely scratches the surface. If you would rather share a screen than share a link, these same-screen multiplayer titles are worth knowing about:

Pac-Man 30th Anniversary

Genre: Arcade | Players: 1–2 on one keyboard

Pac-Man 30th Anniversary is the Google Doodle edition of the maze game, and it works as a genuine head-to-head because both players eat from the same board — the dots are contested rather than individually owned. Arrow keys for one player, WASD for the other, and a layout that rewards whoever thinks two turns ahead.

Gun Mayhem

Genre: Shooter | Players: Up to 4 on one keyboard

Gun Mayhem is the only title we publish that scales to four players sharing a single keyboard, with sixty-plus firearms across twelve maps. The objective is to knock rivals off the stage rather than out-DPS them, which inverts the usual shooter logic and puts the map edge in a very different light. Tips for Better Multiplayer Browser Gaming

  • Wired connection beats Wi-Fi: For any real-time multiplayer game, a wired ethernet connection reduces latency compared to Wi-Fi. IO games are designed to be tolerant of moderate latency, but in close competitive situations, a few milliseconds can matter.
  • Private rooms for friends: Shell Shockers and Smash Karts both let you start a match with people you know rather than hoping you land in the same public lobby. Use it whenever you're playing with a specific group.
  • Learn from spectating: After dying in a game that offers a spectator mode, watch the player who eliminated you. Observing how better players move, position, and make decisions teaches faster than any guide.
  • Play at off-peak hours for beginners: Multiplayer servers are fuller and more competitive on weekday evenings and weekends. If you're new to a game and want to practise against less experienced opponents, weekday mornings tend to have lighter traffic and therefore lower average skill levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are multiplayer browser games free?

Every game on this list is free to play without any purchase required. Some offer optional cosmetic purchases that don't affect gameplay. The core competitive experience is always available at no cost.

Can I play these with friends in different countries?

Yes — all of these games are internet-based, so distance between players doesn't prevent you from playing together. You may notice higher latency if players are connecting from different continents, but Tic Tac Toe IO is essentially unaffected by latency because a move never depends on real-time reaction. Real-time action games like Shell Shockers and Smash Karts perform best when all players are in the same geographic region.

How many players do I need to play these games?

None of these games require you to bring your own players — they all have active public lobbies with real players to match against. That said, the number of people you have available changes which one suits you. Tic Tac Toe IO and Slither.io work equally well as a one-against-the-world duel or as a private match with friends, while Smash Karts is the one that gets noticeably better with a group of three or four.
