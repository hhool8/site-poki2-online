What Is Golf Puzzle?

Golf Puzzle is a mini-golf brain-teaser that blends physics with clever course design. Each hole features bumpers, ramps, teleporters, and moving obstacles that you must account for before swinging. The goal is simple — sink the ball in as few strokes as possible — but the layouts reward careful thinking over brute force.

How to Play

Click and drag away from the ball to set power and direction; a dotted trajectory line shows your predicted shot. Release to hit. Navigate around hazards, use walls as banks, and land the ball in the cup. Par varies per hole, and a lower stroke count earns a higher star rating.

Controls

  • Click + Drag — set shot direction and power
  • Release — swing

Tips

Use the minimum power needed — overshooting bounces the ball out of position. Study moving obstacles and time your shot to pass through gaps. Bank shots off straight walls are more predictable than curved surfaces, so plan routes through flat sections whenever possible.

Course Physics

Golf Puzzle uses simplified 2D physics: gravity, friction, and elastic collisions. Bumpers deflect the ball at predictable angles. Ramps accelerate the ball on launch. Teleporters instantly relocate the ball to a paired teleporter. Moving obstacles (rotating arms, sliding walls) require timing the shot to pass through gaps.

Each hole has a "par" — typically 1–6 strokes depending on length and obstacles. Lower stroke counts earn higher star ratings. Three stars on every hole unlocks the next course pack.

Strategy

Use the maximum-power prediction line. The dotted line shows where the ball will go at full power. Plan shots to end near the cup, not at it.

Time moving obstacles. Sliding walls move on predictable timers. Wait for the gap to align before shooting — rushing creates chaos.

Bank shots are friends. Direct shots are sometimes impossible due to wall geometry. Banking off side walls extends your shot pool significantly.

Bank Shot Mastery

Bank shots are the most powerful tool in Golf Puzzle. A well-executed 3-wall bank travels further than a direct shot at the same power because each bank converts speed into directional precision. Plan shots around 1–2 banks rather than force direct trajectories that often fail on the first attempt.

Sliding walls and rotating arms have predictable movement cycles. Most obstacles cycle every 3–5 seconds. Wait one full cycle before committing to a shot — you'll see the gap pattern and can plan your shot through the moving window with confidence. Minimum power wins. Overshooting moves the ball past the cup and creates ricochets that land nowhere near the target. Use only the power needed to reach the cup, then add 10% margin for friction variation across different hole materials.
