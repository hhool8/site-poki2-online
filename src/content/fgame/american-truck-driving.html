What Is American Truck Driving?

American Truck Driving is a 3D driving simulation. Take the wheel of a heavy-duty truck across American highways, navigate traffic and weather, and deliver cargo on time across long-haul routes.

How to Play

Use WASD or arrow keys to steer and accelerate. Follow the GPS route on the minimap to reach your destination. Stay in your lane, observe speed limits on tight turns, and brake early for sharp corners — trucks take longer to stop than cars.

Controls

  • W / ↑ — accelerate
  • S / ↓ — brake / reverse
  • A / D or ← → — steer

Tips

Slow down before turns — a loaded truck has serious momentum. Use the minimap constantly to anticipate junctions early. Completing deliveries without damage earns bonus cash for upgrades.

Realistic Truck Physics

American Truck Driving simulates the mass and momentum of an 18-wheeler hauling tens of thousands of pounds of cargo. Braking distances are dramatically longer than in a car — even at highway speeds, you need several seconds to bring a fully loaded truck to a stop. Steering is also weightier: small inputs change direction slowly, but oversteer in tight corners can swing the trailer into a jackknife that takes effort to recover from.

Cargo weight affects everything: acceleration, braking, fuel economy, and cornering grip. A fully loaded truck is slower uphill and burns more fuel, but delivers more money on completion. Empty runs are faster but earn less — strategic players balance the two.

Routes and Progression

Coast-to-coast highway system. The map covers multiple American regions: Midwest plains, Rocky Mountain passes, Pacific Coast highways, and Gulf Coast bayous. Each region introduces new hazards — Mountain passes have steep grades and altitude penalties, the Gulf has heavy rain, the Pacific Coast has hairpin corners.

Time-of-day cycle. Day, dusk, and night all affect visibility and traffic density. Night driving has reduced visibility but lighter traffic. Dusk is often the trickiest because of glare.

Weather dynamic. Rain reduces grip on asphalt, especially in mountain curves. Snow is restricted to northern deliveries and significantly slows handling. Fog appears on a few routes and reduces visibility to a small cone around the truck.

Damage model. Hitting guardrails, lampposts, or other vehicles reduces your delivery bonus and requires a repair stop. Major crashes (e.g. jackknifing the trailer) mean a tow and a wasted run.

Pro Tips

Use the engine brake. Downshifting on long descents saves your service brakes from overheating — overheated brakes fail on later mountain routes.

Plan your gear before hills. Don't enter a long uphill grade in too high a gear — you'll lug the engine and stall on the steepest sections.

Watch the weight. Heavier cargo earns more, but if you're just learning the routes, start with lighter loads so mistakes don't hurt as much.

Save for the AWD truck. The all-wheel-drive truck unlocks at level 8 and unlocks the most lucrative mountain routes without slippage penalties.

FAQ

Q: Is American Truck Driving browser-based?
A: Yes — fully playable in your browser without download. Performance is best on desktop with a keyboard.
Q: Can I use a steering wheel or gamepad?
A: Most browsers detect compatible steering wheels via WebHID; gamepads work through standard mapping.
Q: How long are typical routes?
A: Short intercity routes take 5–10 minutes. Coast-to-coast deliveries can take 30+ minutes across real-time driving.
Q: Is there a fast-travel option?
A: No — the entire experience is about the drive itself. There's no skip-feature.
