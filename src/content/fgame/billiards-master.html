What Is Billiards Master?

Billiards Master is a realistic pool simulation with multiple game modes — 8-ball, 9-ball, and practice. It features accurate ball physics, spin control, and a full shot-power system for both casual and competitive play.

How to Play

Aim the cue by moving your mouse around the cue ball. Click and drag backward to set power, then release to shoot. Apply spin by clicking the spin indicator on the cue ball graphic — top-spin, back-spin, and side-spin all change how the cue ball reacts after contact.

Controls

  • Mouse Aim — direction of shot
  • Click + Drag — set shot power
  • Spin Pad — apply English / spin

Tips

Always plan two shots ahead — potting a ball counts for nothing if the cue ball ends up snookered. Back-spin is the most useful technique early on; it stops the cue ball dead after contact, giving you precise position control.

Physics and Spin

Billiards Master applies real-world spin effects to the cue ball. Applying top-spin (a forward roll) makes the cue ball continue forward after contact with the object ball, useful for following the path of the ball you just sank. Applying back-spin (draw) makes the cue ball reverse direction after contact, ideal for position play when you need to back off the rail. Side-spin (English) deflects the cue ball's path during travel and after collision. Mastering these three spins is the difference between casual potting and consistent positional play.

Each spin level costs a small percentage of shot power — heavy spin reduces effective transfer of momentum to the object ball. A common mistake is cranking the spin knob to maximum on every shot.

Strategy

Plan two shots ahead. Pot a ball, then plan what position the cue ball needs to be in for the next shot. Pros always play position first and potting second.

Use the diamond system. The rails are marked with diamonds. Counting diamonds helps aim bank shots off multiple cushions — useful when no direct shot is available.

Avoid kicking unless you have to. Kick shots (where the cue ball must bounce off multiple rails to reach a ball) have low potting odds — look for safer alternatives first.

Practice straight-in shots first. Most amateur players lose more points on positional play than on potting. Drill straight shots to internalise the correct power for short vs long distances.
